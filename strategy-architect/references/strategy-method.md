# Strategy method

## The kernel

Use three linked elements:

1. **Diagnosis:** the critical obstacle or opportunity, framed precisely enough to guide a decision.
2. **Guiding policy:** the approach chosen to address it; this is a policy, not a slogan.
3. **Coherent actions:** a limited set of mutually reinforcing moves that enact the policy.

If an action does not follow from the diagnosis and policy, remove it or revise the logic.

## The two-sided strategy

Express both perspectives.

| Perspective | Problem | Goal | Solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business | What prevents the organisation from achieving its outcome? | What measurable outcome matters? | What will it change or enable? |
| Audience | What job, friction, anxiety, aspiration, or trade-off is unresolved? | What progress does the audience seek? | Why will the offer or communication help? |

Turn the two perspectives into one concise paragraph. Aim for a chain of logic rather than a string of components.

## Insight test

An insight should make the reader see the decision differently. Test it:

- Does it reveal an unspoken human truth, contradiction, or overlooked constraint?
- Does it reframe the problem or unlock a surprising but credible response?
- Does it make the proposed direction more specific?
- Would it remain useful if the brand name were removed? If yes, it may be only a category observation.

## Writing the strategy

Draft three alternatives before selecting one. Useful structures include:

- “For [priority audience], [brand/product] can win by [choice] because [evidence-based reason].”
- “Get [audience] to [progress] by showing/doing [meaningful reframing or offer].”
- “It is not [category convention]; it is [new lens].”

Keep the final strategy short enough to repeat accurately. The single-minded proposition should be a strategic stimulus, not an advertising headline.

## Choice and leverage

Seek leverage rather than activity volume: a bottleneck to unlock, an advantage to deepen, a change to ride, a pivotal audience or moment to focus on, or a system of actions that works only together. State what will be deprioritised. Distinguish a proximate objective — challenging but achievable with current capabilities — from a distant aspiration.
