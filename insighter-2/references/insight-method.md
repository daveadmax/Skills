# Insight method

Use this concise working method when developing the final sentence. It is adapted from the user-provided *Mastering Insights: The All-In-One Guide* by Mark Pollard / Sweathead; retain it as internal method guidance, not as a substitute for current research.

## Definition

Aim for an unspoken human truth that casts new light on the particular human problem behind the brief. An insight is not the source material: facts, statistics, trends, reports, claims, implications, and recommendations can support an insight, but do not replace it.

## Find productive material

Collect concrete signals: observed behavior, precise wording, confessions, frustrations, exceptions, cultural examples, and research findings. Search beneath each useful signal:

- Why might this be true?
- What makes it happen?
- What is the opposite, and when does that happen?
- What is public performance versus private feeling?
- What would feel risky, embarrassing, or self-contradictory to say aloud?

Treat a vivid quote or odd statistic as a lead, not proof. Look for the broader pattern and the problem it reframes.

## Make the leap

Bring together two ideas that do not normally meet, but make each other more useful when combined. The sentence should contain a turn: people assume one thing, but the deeper reality is another.

Use these drafting structures privately; do not mechanically expose them in the final answer:

- `X is Y`
- `X is not A; it is B`
- `People think X, but Y`
- `When X happens, Y happens too`
- `X succeeds despite Y`
- `X fails unless Y`

## Pressure-test

Before choosing a sentence, check:

1. Could an affected person recognize themselves without feeling patronized?
2. Does it change how the brief’s human problem is seen?
3. Is the surprise a meaningful reframe rather than wordplay?
4. Is it short enough to repeat and fertile enough to inspire multiple executions?
5. Can its key inference be supported, or should it be presented as a hypothesis?

Rewrite in plain language until the thought, not the phrasing, carries the force.
