# Strategy outputs

## Strategy on a page

Use this compact structure when the user needs a decision-ready view:

| Element | Write |
|---|---|
| Business problem | The most consequential obstacle, not every symptom. |
| Business goal | A measurable outcome and timeframe. |
| Priority audience | A behaviour- or need-defined group. |
| Audience problem | The friction or unresolved job in the audience’s language. |
| Insight | The reframe that makes a new response feel necessary. |
| Brand/product truth | A credible asset, proof point, or right to play. |
| Strategic choice | Where to play and how to win. |
| Single-minded proposition | The memorable thought that links insight and truth. |
| Coherent actions | The few moves that make the strategy real. |
| Measures | Outcome, leading indicators, and learning cadence. |

## Creative territories

For each territory, provide:

- a name and one-sentence premise;
- the tension or insight it exploits;
- why the brand has permission to play there;
- what it could look, sound, or do like;
- two illustrative executions, clearly marked as examples rather than the strategy.

Make territories meaningfully different: one may reframe the category, one may dramatise the audience tension, and one may make a distinctive product or organisational truth famous.

## Case-study analysis

Use a compact pattern:

1. Context and original challenge.
2. Diagnosis and pivotal strategic choice.
3. Coherent actions or expression.
4. Evidence of result and limits of attribution.
5. Transferable lesson and where it would not apply.

## Workshop plan

For a planning session, set a concrete decision and produce a 60–120 minute agenda:

1. Align on outcome and non-negotiables.
2. Review evidence and assumptions.
3. Write individual problem statements before debate.
4. Generate competing insights and strategic choices.
5. Select with explicit criteria: impact, distinctiveness, credibility, feasibility, and measurability.
6. Convert the winner into a strategy on a page and assign validation actions.
