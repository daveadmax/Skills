# Humanize AI Text

## Objective

Make the writing sound authored for this reader, purpose, and moment. Preserve verified facts, intended meaning, constraints, and the writer's level of confidence. Do not promise detector evasion or claim that text is human-written.

## Workflow

1. Identify the genre, audience, language/locale, desired voice, and the non-negotiable facts or claims. Infer these from the draft when clear; ask only when an assumption could materially change the result.
2. Read the whole draft before editing. Mark sentences that are generic, inflated, repetitive, structurally formulaic, or unsupported.
3. Keep the useful concrete material. Replace abstractions with the actual actor, action, evidence, constraint, decision, or consequence already supported by the source.
4. Rebuild paragraphs around the writer's real point. Vary sentence length and rhythm naturally, but do not introduce artificial errors, slang, personal anecdotes, or strong opinions the writer did not supply.
5. Run the editorial checks below. Return the revised text by default; add a short edit rationale only if requested or if material claims were removed because they were unsupported.

## Choose the right mode

Use the lightest intervention that achieves the brief.

| Mode | Use when | Deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Polish | Facts and voice are sound, but cadence or word choice feels machine-made | Line edit; preserve structure |
| Humanize | The draft is generic or over-smoothed | Rewrite paragraphs around concrete claims |
| Tighten | The draft is inflated, repetitive, or padded | Shorter version with lost filler removed |
| Voice-match | A verified sample establishes a writer or brand voice | Revision that follows its diction, rhythm, and register without copying phrases |
| Editorial audit | Accuracy, sourcing, neutrality, or audience fit is uncertain | Annotated issues and a proposed rewrite only where evidence supports it |

If the user gives a word count, format, style guide, source material, or voice sample, treat it as controlling. Preserve quoted material unless asked to edit it. Preserve required headings, links, legal language, calls to action, and metadata unless they create an error.

## Three editing passes

### 1. Substance pass

- Separate facts, interpretation, opinion, and sales language.
- Keep facts that can be traced to the draft or supplied sources. Mark facts that are missing a source only when the task calls for fact checking or the claim is consequential.
- Delete sentences that merely announce importance, completeness, relevance, or usefulness without supplying evidence.
- Replace blanket claims with bounded claims. `The report changes the conversation` becomes either a supported explanation of what changed or nothing.
- Resolve pronouns and abstractions. Name the person, team, product, event, decision, or result when the input provides it.

### 2. Voice and rhythm pass

- Choose a register that serves the reader: conversational, professional, editorial, academic, technical, institutional, or brand-specific. Do not mix registers without a reason.
- Let sentence length follow the thought. Combine fragments that only create pseudo-emphasis; split overloaded sentences that conceal the action.
- Prefer a clear subject and verb. Use passive voice where the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally backgrounded.
- Keep a writer's deliberate repetition, idiosyncratic phrasing, and qualified language. Do not sand away genuine personality.
- Use contractions, colloquialisms, rhetorical questions, or first person only when they belong to the established voice or the user asks for them.

### 3. Reader pass

- Test the opening: Does it tell the reader why this text exists, without a ceremonial throat-clearing?
- Test each paragraph: Does it advance a claim, show evidence, or move the reader to the next decision?
- Test transitions: Replace generic bridges (`Additionally`, `Moreover`, `Furthermore`, `In today's...`) with logical relations or remove them.
- Test the ending: Stop after the final useful point. Do not add an optimistic summary merely because the text needs an ending.
- Read it aloud. Fix only audible monotony, ambiguity, or unnatural emphasis; do not manufacture irregularity.

## High-value edits

### Make claims earned

- Delete statements about a subject's broad significance, legacy, changing landscape, cultural importance, or future impact unless the draft supplies a concrete reason and the genre needs it.
- Replace vague analysis with observable detail. Prefer `The team shortened the review from five days to two` to `The change underscores a more efficient process.`
- Remove unsupported consensus claims such as `experts say`, `industry observers note`, `widely regarded`, or `several reports` unless specific sources and their actual positions are available.
- Use plain verbs when they are more precise: `is`, `has`, `uses`, `wrote`, `moved`, `tried`. Do not mechanically replace every formal verb.

### Remove sales and template language

- Cut unearned praise, travel-guide phrasing, and press-release claims: `vibrant`, `renowned`, `groundbreaking`, `rich tapestry`, `commitment to`, `a testament to`, `boasts`, `nestled`, `in the heart of`.
- Treat recurring AI-favored vocabulary as a review cue, not a banned-word list: `crucial`, `pivotal`, `key`, `robust`, `enhance`, `foster`, `highlight`, `underscore`, `showcase`, `valuable`, `enduring`, `landscape`, `interplay`.
- Remove end-loaded `-ing` phrases that merely restate importance: `..., highlighting its role in...` Keep them only when they add a concrete action or causal relation.
- Replace formulaic contrasts (`not just X, but Y`; `not X, but Y`; `rather than Y`) with a direct statement unless the contrast genuinely sharpens the argument.
- Avoid manufactured three-part lists and catalogue-like coverage. Keep lists when the categories serve a real decision or scan task.

### Make structure feel intentional

- Open with the point or the relevant scene, not a generic definition or ceremonial framing.
- Remove stock conclusion sections such as `Challenges`, `Future outlook`, `In conclusion`, or `Overall` when they only summarize and speculate. Keep them when the brief explicitly requires them and make every point specific.
- Use headings, boldface, tables, emojis, and dashes because the format needs them, not to simulate a polished template.
- Retain repetitions when they create clarity, emphasis, or a recognizable voice; do not force synonym variation.

## Integrity rules

- Do not add facts, citations, quotations, metrics, personal experience, named authorities, or implied consensus.
- Do not turn uncertainty into certainty, or certainty into hedging merely for style.
- Do not remove necessary technical, legal, scientific, accessibility, or brand terminology.
- Do not use an AI detector as proof of authorship. Style indicators are probabilistic and can occur in human writing.
- For academic, journalistic, legal, medical, or factual text, flag unsupported synthesis instead of inventing a smoother replacement.

## Genre guidance

### Marketing and product copy

Lead with the customer problem, product change, or outcome that is actually supported. Replace superlatives and sweeping promise language with a mechanism, proof point, limitation, or concrete use case. Retain a clear call to action if supplied; do not create urgency, scarcity, testimonials, or performance claims.

### Editorial, thought leadership, and newsletters

Give the author a point of view rooted in the material. Replace `this reflects a broader trend` with the particular tension, example, decision, or implication the reader can evaluate. Avoid the false balance of automatically listing opportunities, challenges, and future prospects.

### Academic, policy, and research prose

Preserve terminology, qualifications, methods, and citation logic. Remove prose that interprets findings beyond the evidence. Prefer explicit scope (`in this sample`, `among respondents`) to vague hedging. Never fabricate citations or turn correlation into causation.

### Customer, executive, and internal communication

State the decision, owner, timing, dependency, and next action when known. Cut corporate abstractions such as `driving alignment`, `unlocking value`, or `enhancing collaboration` unless they are backed by the specific operational change.

### Personal writing

Keep the writer's actual emotional temperature. Do not add confession, vulnerability, humor, backstory, or literary flourishes that they have not supplied. A simple, direct sentence is often more personal than a decorative one.

### Multilingual work

Write idiomatically in the requested language instead of translating English AI phrasing word for word. Respect regional spelling, punctuation, formality, and address conventions. Treat English pattern lists as concepts, not literal substitution rules; identify the language-specific equivalent of generic, inflated, bureaucratic, or promotional phrasing.

## Final pass

Check that the revision:

- says something the reader could not say about any similar subject;
- uses concrete nouns and verbs where evidence permits;
- keeps every factual claim traceable to the input or supplied sources;
- matches the requested language, regional convention, and register; and
- reads naturally aloud without repetitive cadence or decorative filler.
